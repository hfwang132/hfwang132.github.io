I had long heard of 《Sky: Children of the Light》, so after finishing my exams and having nothing to do, I tried it out. Since late at night happens to be the time when inspiration and feelings most readily burst forth, I will write a post-play impression (?)

Overall, the game feels like it borrowed from AER Memories of Old; both the art design and the setting are quite similar. First, both are set in the sky. Second, both have settings along the lines of “the world used to be prosperous, but later fell into ruin because of some upheaval; the player must obtain salvation through a pilgrimage, tracing the memories of ancestors, and so on.” Finally, their visual styles are particularly alike, though Sky is somewhat softer and has excellent anti-aliasing. In short, they are both exquisite works of art.

But compared with the single-player puzzle game AER, Sky places its emphasis on interaction between players, while its puzzle elements are approximately zero. In the game, you will encounter many other unfamiliar players, but you can become friends with them by giving candles (equivalent to the game's universal currency). Interestingly, players do not have their own IDs, but can name their friends. Without IDs, players can largely avoid making preconceived judgments about others. You can also hold hands with friends (literally hold hands) and move together; if you get separated, you can call out to find your friend's location. Friends play an important role: for example, some places require two people to hold down buttons at the same time in order to open a door. A year is actually just an artificial division imbued with meaning. There is no difference in temporal significance between crossing into a new year and living through each present minute and second. Some people like to say they will do something until next year (for example, my roommate said he would keep his erection until next year); some summarize one division of the past, make wishes and set plans for one division of the future. But when looking back at the starting point from the end of that division, how many resolutions have truly been achieved? A year is, after all, too long. I think progress need not be measured in years. Make the most of the present; on the foundation of aspirations, think more about the present or the near future, and that should probably be enough. After all, none of us would be arrogant enough to commit a whole set of plans to the coming 365 days. As Zixia said: “Learn broadly and hold fast to your purpose, inquire earnestly and reflect on what is close at hand; therein lies benevolence.”

...I have not played games for a long time since the semester started. Tonight, after finishing my review, I had nothing to do, so I downloaded and played a game, wanting to reunite with the feeling of gaming. Yet I found that not only did it not bring me satisfaction, but after every failure it made me feel anger, irritation, and regret. When I put down the game, I felt a deep emptiness and loneliness again.

Although I do not think all types of games should be condemned wholesale, it seems that some types of games truly only make you unable to extricate yourself from brief excitement and getting carried away, as well as frustration and unwillingness to accept defeat after failure, ultimately exhausting all your energy.

Games can be seasoning for life or an artistic pursuit; they can be an outlet for pressure and a pleasant experience, or they can be wonderful stories and unforgettable adventures. But games can likewise degenerate into opium for the spirit and demons that disrupt life, into inferior products for making money and tools for controlling the brain. This is related not only to the quality of the game itself, but also to the choices of those who participate in it.

So, make games serve yourself rather than being enslaved by them. That is probably the attitude one should have toward games.

Last night, I dreamed that I went through an unforgettable adventure with a group of friends who seemed to have known one another for a long time. It was as though a very, very long time had passed in the dream—so long that the bonds between us had become so close that they could never be forgotten.

But when I woke up, I found that I could not remember a single one of their names, nor could I clearly remember their faces—it turned out that I had never known this group of people in the real world.

That feeling was like heartbreak, though I have never experienced heartbreak.

But why should the world after waking up be real? Why say that the world after waking is more real than the world in dreams? Could the world after waking not be a dream? Could the world in dreams not be the real one?
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Then I suddenly thought of the essence of cognition: the active reflection of the subject upon the object on the basis of practice. On the one hand, the reflective nature of cognition is a basic defining characteristic of human cognition. The reflective nature of cognition means that human cognition must take objective things as its prototype and model. No matter how complex the forms in which human cognition manifests itself may be, it is ultimately a reflection of objective things. On the other hand, the active reflection of cognition is creative. Cognition is an active, creative activity in thought, rather than a simple, direct depiction by the subject of objective things or a mirror-like reproduction of the original object.
