I arrived in Singapore on July 31. Today marks my second week abroad in Singapore.

I was very excited during the first week, exploring all around Singapore~ trying to step out of my comfort zone and interact with all kinds of people. But this quickly made me depressed—because I encountered all sorts of people who were fundamentally not on the same wavelength as me.

Many of them come from very wealthy families, and studying abroad means learning while having fun. I met people from Taiwan, Spain, Mexico, Malaysia, and Indonesia, and their lifestyles were way too wild for me (drinking, casual sex). I could not stand it. Once the drinking got going, they started playing a game similar to Truth or Dare—if you had not done a certain thing, you had to take two penalty drinks. The questions went like this: “Has your fuck buddy ever fallen asleep during sex?” and “Have you ever put xx into xx?” I had originally wanted to practice English with foreign friends, but in the end I could only find an excuse and leave in a hurry.

Even the people from China did not make me feel much better. There was a girl from Sichuan in the music department whose rent was three times mine (not to mention tuition); she spent lavishly, treating her parents' money like dirt. As someone barely managing to live independently on a full scholarship, I simply could not find anything to talk about with them.

Among people from different countries, those from Northern Europe, Western Europe, and the United States made the best impression on me. They were generally more poised, well-mannered, and fluent speakers (mainly because their English was easy to understand; Southeast Asian English, Southern European English, Eastern European English, and South American English with accents are truly impossible to understand at all). Moreover, you can feel that people from places like Northern Europe, Western Europe, and the United States genuinely came to study and work, rather than just fool around. My conversations with a Swiss guy went like this: “Why did you choose mathematics rather than physics?” and “Which area of mathematics are you interested in?” We had a great time talking.

After being baptized by the first week, I started questioning my life: had the first half of my life been wasted? We East Asians start competing from a young age, but many people from other places do not need to put in the same effort and can still achieve better results. A Malaysian guy was studying at Tsinghua University. I asked him why he chose Tsinghua rather than NUS, and he replied: “I wanted to experience life somewhere farther away.”.. It is clear that other people's lives are incredibly free: pursuing a master's degree is simply about experiencing different parts of the world, not about obtaining a credential to make it easier to find a job. I had heard about many people like this online before, but only after meeting and talking with them face-to-face offline did I experience a real psychological shock.

In the second week, joining the research group deepened my anxiety even more. I began to question what exactly my purpose in pursuing a PhD was: was it for interest? But my family is not well-off, and doing it purely for interest is truly a luxury—one could even say it is irresponsible to my family. Then, am I pursuing a PhD for a higher degree so that I can make money in the future? But that runs completely counter to the spirit of scientific research. After thinking about it for a week, I feel that I may now have a preliminary answer: only when someone likes their work/research can they truly achieve something, and then perhaps money naturally ceases to be a problem. But then again, it is best to do research only if your family is financially well-off. If your family has no money—while the people around you have all started earning money to support their families, you are still struggling away at research—it really does not feel good! What I envy, resent, and hate the most is people whose families are wealthy and who do research without having to worry about any financial pressure at all—research is purely just an interest for them. Damn it, I want to become that kind of person too!
